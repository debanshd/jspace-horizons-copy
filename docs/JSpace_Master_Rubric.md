# J-SPACE / LEARNED INTELLIGENCE — THE 100-POINT COUNCIL RELEASE STANDARD

**Version:** 2.0 — MASTERWORK STANDARD · **Date:** 10 November 2026 · **Target:** a technically unassailable, intellectually revolutionary discovery whose conceptual influence plausibly endures to 2036 and beyond.

**Purpose.** Evaluate ideas, pre-registered experiments, completed results, figures, code, and manuscripts against a **Michelangelo-level scientific masterwork** standard. The aim is not merely to pass peer review, or even receive an oral or best-paper award. The aim is to discover something so profound, simple in retrospect, predictive, and explanatory that it **changes what the field thinks is possible**, launches a line of research others cannot ignore, and is taught or debated for a decade or more. Aim for work of the intellectual magnitude that could credibly interest *MIT Technology Review* or major public science journalism, while never treating attention, award, celebrity, or institutional prestige as proof. **No rubric can guarantee instant acceptance, a career outcome, public coverage, or prizes.** The test is whether the *science* would deserve unusually broad attention if rigorously established.

**Source basis.** Consolidates the project's recorded requirements, the original named mechanistic and cognitive-science council, the expanded cognitive panel, and the archived P01–P16 theoretical objections. Named researchers remain intellectual reference points drawn from their published work: **this document does not claim their personal authorship, voting, review, or endorsement**. The framework instead requires the project team to integrate these independent challenges into one coherent scientific creation, rather than paper over disagreements. The weights and aspirational masterwork standard are editorial decisions for this project.

## THE OVERRIDING ASPIRATION: SCIENTIFIC MASTERWORK, NOT TECHNICAL COMPLETENESS

**Michelangelo test.** Imagine that every formula, experiment, figure, and sentence forms one necessary part of a single intellectual structure. Remove one pillar and the explanatory structure weakens; remove the decorative jargon and the structure becomes clearer. The paper should feel *inevitable after the discovery*, while its central insight would have been extremely difficult to see beforehand. Technical complexity should not be mistaken for depth, and flowery prose should not be mistaken for beauty.

**The five non-negotiable masterwork questions:**

1. **Revelation:** What previously invisible fact about learned intelligence becomes impossible to unsee after Figure 1? State it as a simple, counterintuitive sentence, and identify the prior theory or informed expectation it contradicts.
2. **Field elevation:** What can the community *now explain, predict, design, or falsify* that it could not before? Name three consequential new research directions and one concrete future experiment the discovery makes possible.
3. **Conceptual beauty:** Is there one compact principle that unifies phenomena previously treated as unrelated, without hiding contradictions or adding unnecessary terminology? Can an expert reconstruct the key argument from a single diagram and a small number of exact definitions?
4. **Historical staying power:** Would an expert in 2036 still need the principle even if the original models, architectures, benchmarks, and brand name disappear? Could it become a textbook concept, a standard experimental technique, or a genuine theoretical constraint?
5. **World-facing intelligibility and authorial signature:** Can a truthful, non-hyped account make a serious non-specialist say 'I did not know machines could work that way'—and can reviewers recognize the unmistakably clear intellectual contribution of the authors? This is about the merit and communication of the discovery, **not a guarantee of media coverage or professional status**.

**Ambition filter:** A technically perfect but merely incremental paper fails. A sensational but unsupported claim fails. A complicated paper that cannot express its central revelation clearly fails. A result that merely uses a new dataset or modality fails. A paper that makes the authors famous *only in its marketing copy* fails. Scientific truth and extraordinary vision are **both required**, with independent veto power.

**Council composition principle:** The final theory must be **jointly shaped by the strongest objections** from mechanistic interpretability, mathematical causal inference, cognitive science, philosophy, and multimodal research. Independent reviewers must be able to change the thesis, the choice of decisive experiment, the mechanism, and the presentation—not merely attach endorsements. Counterarguments that survive remain explicit. No consensus or signatures are invented.

## A. How to score without gaming

- **Exactly 30 weighted criteria; exactly 100 points. Masterwork/field-redefinition receive 45 of the 100 points, while mathematical and empirical validity retain independent vetoes.** Each item receives 0%, 50%, or 100% of its assigned weight. Awarding anything else requires dated written scoring anchors and a reproducible justification before reviewing the candidate. Every 100% requires a specific artifact or decisive observation. No credit for impressive rhetoric.
- Use the *same weights* for **Proposal Quality (PQ/100)** and **Discovery Evidence (DE/100)**, recorded separately. **PQ** judges whether a proposed theory and its precommitted decisive tests are logically sound, genuinely novel, well controlled and realistically executable; a not-yet-run idea is not automatically disqualified from PQ=100. **DE** judges what has been established by actual data/proofs/code. A plan alone is not evidence of success; DE is not called 100 on plans.
- **0%:** absent, wrong, counterexample already known, or decisive confound unaddressed. **50%:** credible but incomplete/partial support, missing an essential distinctiveness test or independent verification. **100%:** passes the full-stated anchor for that stage (PQ: fully designed and justified; DE: actually observed/verified, except design and reporting items where correct performance is observed).
- **Ten noncompensatory gates** below each have RED/YELLOW/GREEN. Any RED blocks promotion, however high the score. YELLOW is not a passing gate; every applicable gate must be GREEN. Never call a candidate “council-approved” just because its mean score is high.
- An explicit **N/A** is possible only for a truly unclaimed, noncentral assertion (e.g., subjective awareness). Core tasks—novelty, theory, causal identification, falsification, generalization, accuracy, inference discipline—are never N/A. An unused item retains its full weight via a *predeclared equally difficult substitute*, approved before results, never by silently dropping it. Here the rubric assumes a J-space/multimodal paper, so exact J and real-modality validation are central.
- Evidence grades refer to **validated claim support**, not numbers from a manuscript. A screenshot, selective run, assistant speculation, or model-generated literature review is not independent verification. Keep every negative and failed gate in an append-only ledger.

## C. Ten noncompensatory release gates: masterwork AND truth

**No averaging away a fatal flaw.** A candidate with 99/100 and one RED gate is rejected. GREEN requires the strongest available evidence appropriate to the proposal or completed-discovery stage; YELLOW means unresolved and is never a release clearance. Reviewers must record the single cheapest observation that could turn a gate RED.

## E. Mandatory technical controls and evidence preservation

### E1. Causal variables to disentangle

A minimal semantic schema is `o` (actual object/scene), `q` (task/question), `b=q(o)` (task-specific property or truth), `π` (arbitrary answer-code mapping), `a=π(b)` (response plan), `s` (source/provenance), `m` (modality), `t` (timing/token position) and `c` (context). A single donor/receiver manipulation is **underidentified** if changing `b` necessarily changes `a`, `q` or `s`.

Essential intervention arms: same `b` / different donor `a`; different `b` / same donor `a`; same `o` / new `q`; same semantic content / new `s` and modality; inverted receiver `π`; image-before-question vs question-before-image with causal masking respected; neutral carrier before mapping; natural read-site knockout with restore; zero-dose/self-donor; adjacent-site and random negative; J/complement/random/PCA/full-state; and fresh-held-out settings. Only introduce a control when its factor is meaningfully supported by the model's pipeline; never claim that an invalid intervention is a valid causal manipulation.

Use both log-probability margins and decoded behavior; measure systematic errors, not just answer flips. Guard against answer-digit leakage, lexical identity, class imbalance, token position/length, task difficulty, false positives, decoder stochasticity and donor confidence. Predefine practical effect and equivalence margins. Correct for adaptive layer/head scans.

### E2. Why the current record does **not** pass by default

- **R106:** a time-dependent predicate→mapping relay was observed in a particular 4B setting. It does **not** establish a natural general-purpose workspace.
- **R250:** complementary question/template effects occurred in 27B, but truth and answer code were confounded. It does **not** identify an abstract truth representation without additional counterbalanced donors.
- **Visual name/last-letter:** the gap varies substantially with model size; it cannot justify a universal lexical barrier.
- **J-related tests:** a dense rank-256 projection is not the original sparse nonnegative J algorithm; some complementary spaces and matched random directions work.
- **Source monitoring:** earlier source-monitoring head claims were withdrawn; answer/source reporting must be re-established from ground truth.
- **Video/audio:** failed competence or mismatched video processing cannot establish presence or absence of a multimodal pathway.
- **Cross-model transfer:** different prompts/tasks/layers across checkpoints do not establish generalization. A precommitted held-out second family is essential for a broad claim.

The rubric must not claim any of these observations as a discovery or interpret them as proof of absence. They are historical prompts for stronger controls and can be retained in a verified ledger.

### E3. File/code hygiene and feasibility

Keep `RECORD`/immutable experiment logs lossless and preserve all failures. Do not alter a running experiment or original result file in the course of an audit; use versioned branches, copies and hashes. Never inspect or disclose secret-bearing files such as `.env`; do not publish, post, share, or submit any artifact without explicit user approval. Validate tensor shape, layer indices, causal direction, positional alignment, model mode (prefill/decode), attention cache, model-specific multimodal preprocessing, deterministic reruns, OOM recovery and exact reset of intervention hooks. Prefer a decisive T4-feasible pilot when possible; if impossible, include a realistic hardware, runtime and checkpoint plan and failure fallback. A 'cheap' pilot is not acceptable if it sacrifices the necessary causal discrimination.

## F0. The council's atelier: making one original paper together

**The council is not a crowd of scorecards.** It must produce a singular paper that integrates intellectual strengths without pretending that every view is compatible. The project team acts as the authors; reviewer names designate lines of scrutiny and contribution requirements, not actual participation by those individuals.

**Round A — Independent revelations.** Each research perspective proposes one hypothesis that could rewrite a textbook and one existing theory that would fail if it were true. No forced common vocabulary or J-space-first framing. Reject ideas that are merely a known phenomenon with heightened language.

**Round B — Conceptual sculpture.** Put the best ideas into direct competition. Extract the smallest shared explanatory insight **only if** it increases predictive power. Remove concepts that do not change an inference. Preserve incommensurable alternatives as rival hypotheses, never combine them into an unfalsifiable umbrella.

**Round C — Adversarial chiseling.** Every domain makes the proposal stronger by providing a quantitative counterprediction, a kill experiment, and one *constructive* idea that fundamentally improves the theory or experimental design. A reviewer cannot merely praise or repeat a checklist.

**Round D — The artwork and the proof.** Design the central figure and derive the central formal statement together. They must express *the same discovery*. The experiment must distinguish the exact theory from its most dangerous surviving rivals. The figure cannot hide a failed control; the equation cannot make a stronger claim than the data.

**Round E — Public and specialist blind read.** Without viewing the authors' title or pitch, ask a mechanistic expert, cognitive scientist, theorist, unrelated ML expert and an educated non-specialist: 'What new idea did you learn? Why was it surprising? What could it predict? What would falsify it?' Agreement on the **actual** discovery matters; enthusiasm alone does not.

**Round F — The decade test.** Remove the names of current models, J-space, architecture details and publication venue. Write the hypothetical 2036 textbook paragraph, three new research directions, and a technically faithful 120-word public explanation. If there is no substance left, the paper is not field-defining.

**The final council packet:** (a) a one-sentence revelation; (b) what prevailing assumption it overturns; (c) the diagram, theorem and experimental evidence in one chain; (d) rival explanations defeated and left open; (e) a frozen prediction and independent confirmation; (f) the precise author contributions; (g) a 2036 consequence; (h) one clear explanation for a major public science audience; (i) explicit reasons for independent rejection if any. Do not invent expert votes, correspondence, authorship, or endorsements.

## F. Workflow: evaluate without letting the council drift

**Stage 0 — Candidate admission:** one-sentence *revelation*, an explicit field assumption it would overturn, nearest three prior claims, a pair of competing causal models, one counterintuitive prediction, one feasible falsifier, a 2036 textbook consequence, and a one-sketch Figure-1 argument. Stop if no coherent differentiator. Do not generate a glamorous paper title before the scientific claim exists.

**Stage 1 — Proposal score (PQ/100):** score all 30 items as a *research design* with exact data, intervention, quantitative prediction, sample, methods, logistics, confounds and failure boundaries. The council may award PQ=100 to a proposal, but not because it promises a future breakthrough; each prospective criterion must already have a fully credible, executable design. Screen G1–G10 for theoretical/design feasibility, including the masterwork, field-transformation, and public-meaning gates.

**Stage 2 — Decisive pilot:** run only the earliest experiment that can kill the central hypothesis. Lock split/seeds/site-selection rules and forecasts. Verify competence, null controls and intervention exactness before looking at target outcomes. If the pilot fails, preserve it and revise/withdraw rather than retitling.

**Stage 3 — Mechanism:** demand natural writer→reader identification, quantified bypass routes, semantic factorization, independent intervention/restoration, alternate mechanisms and held-out transfer.

**Stage 4 — Prediction and scope:** freeze quantitative theory, independently test held-out tasks, other scales/families, genuine video/audio and training perturbations when claimed. A new model must not be re-explained post hoc and then called confirmation.

**Stage 5 — Manuscript and replication:** reproduce figures from raw data, independent code and cognitive audits, blind-reader figure tests, full prior-art/release search, abstract sentence-by-sentence evidence map. Score DE/100, apply all ten gates and require independent GREEN verdicts.

### Required review output (every round)

1. **Exact thesis and status:** one sentence and PQ/100, DE/100, scored-date, candidate version.
2. **Thirty-row scores:** for each item: 0, half, full; evidence artifact/line/run; strongest surviving objection; cheapest kill test; owner.
3. **Ten gates:** RED/YELLOW/GREEN with explicit evidence and fatal-flaw reason; the revelation, field-transformation, and public-significance gates must be independently justified, not inferred from technical quality.
4. **Three independent verdicts:** Mechanistic identification, Cognitive-theory/explanatory validity, Interdisciplinary field consequence. Each says GREEN/YELLOW/RED separately.
5. **Prior-art comparison:** exact overlap and novelty delta for closest works (method, theorem, mechanism, results, tasks, model family).
6. **Evidence and contradiction ledger:** proven vs inferred vs speculative, including all failed predictions and void arms; source datasets and hash references.
7. **Alternative model tournament:** ≥2 serious countermodels, each with signed and quantitative novel predictions, decision experiment and stop rule.
8. **Experiment spec:** clean ability gate, donor/receiver table, intervention site and timing, expected pattern, matched controls, stats/power, run cost and feasibility.
9. **Figures/claims:** figure-to-claim map, Figure-1 revelation test, graphical blind-reader audit, corrections to headline/abstract, an exact one-page major-science explainer, and a 2036 textbook paragraph.
10. **Council disagreements, intellectual co-creation and final ruling:** every named reviewer/seat gets its domain-specific strongest veto **and one constructive revision** to the theory or test; independent objections must actively improve the common paper, not merely police it. Conflicts are adjudicated by a decisive experiment or left OPEN—never resolved by rhetoric.

**Final law:** A 99/100 result with invalid causal identification is rejected. A perfect technical paper with no profound revelation fails G1/G2.
