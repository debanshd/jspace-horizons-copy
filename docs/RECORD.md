## Literature (13 Oct) — what is taken, what is open

| Prior work | What it shows | What it leaves open (our contribution) |
|---|---|---|
| Gurnee et al. 2026, *Verbalizable representations form a global workspace* ([arXiv 2607.15495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15495)) | J-lens on text; contents reportable, summoned, **held**, used in silent reasoning; coherent in a middle layer band; "on the order of **tens of concepts** at a time"; broadcast widely. | **No non-text input at all.** Capacity measured under instructions to hold; not under load from a scene. |
| Neel Nanda's review ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/zFJ3ZdQwrTWE9jT5S/a-review-of-anthropic-s-global-workspace-paper)) | Scientific claim "overwhelming"; "J-Lens is about predicting subsequent tokens"; wants **reliability / false-positive data** and multi-token extensions; capacity and multimodality not addressed. | A readable-but-not-used demonstration is exactly the false-positive mode he asks about. |
| Neo et al., ICLR 2025 ([arXiv 2410.07149](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.07149)) | **Logit lens** on LLaVA visual tokens: ~24% of object patches decode to the object word at the best layer; object info localized to its patches (ablation). | Only the logit lens; one VLM; no J-lens; no load; no report-position contrast. |
| LatentLens ([arXiv 2602.00462](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00462)) | Logit lens **underestimates** visual-token interpretability; most visual tokens interpretable with a nearest-neighbour lens. | The J-lens — "a principled refinement of the logit lens" — never tried on visual tokens. **Our B2a: 100% of present shapes readable on their own patches with the J-lens.** |
| Zhang et al., CVPR 2025 ([cross-modal information flow](https://mlanthology.org/cvpr/2025/zhang2025cvpr-crossmodal/)) | Attention knockouts: visual info flows into question tokens, then to the last position. | Our causal patch: the question-token state **alone** carries nothing; a later question re-reads the scene tokens. |
| *Do AV-LLMs really see and hear?* CVPR 2026 ([arXiv 2604.02605](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02605)); AudioLens ([2506.05140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05140)); *Can we read the mind of an audio LLM?* ([2608.24958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24958)) | Logit lens at audio tokens: audio semantics at intermediate/late layers; deeper fusion layers privilege vision and suppress audio; spoken-QA answers legible in middle layers on Qwen3-Omni base. | Logit lens only; no J-lens; no load manipulation; no image-vs-sound-vs-word comparison of **where** (token, layer) content becomes verbalizable; no held-vs-planned contrast. |
| GLW models (VanRullen & Kanai 2021; Devillers et al. TNNLS 2024) | Engineered amodal global latent workspaces across modalities. | Whether a *trained omni LLM's* verbalizable space is amodal is untested. Our 4B: picture > text at the report; 27B: equal. |

**Contribution, stated for a reviewer:** the first J-space study with non-verbal input. (i) *Where* a sound or picture becomes
verbalizable: at its own tokens, in a modality-dependent layer band (J-lens vs logit lens quantified on image and audio tokens — the
workspace paper's methodological claim extended beyond text). (ii) *What* the report position holds under load across modalities:
what is about to be said; nothing under a count question; the held-for-later test decides whether a multimodal workspace maintains
content ahead of need (the paper's "held" claim, tested with pictures and sounds). (iii) *Readable ≠ used* as J-lens reliability data,
with dimension-fair controls, or dropped if the control shows the split is uninformative. (iv) Size: reportable-at-once shrinks from
4B to 27B. All with preregistered rules, nulls, perception gates and a scene bootstrap.

## E-pilot rules (frozen 14 Oct 00:10; amendments dated inside)

## Rival hypotheses and what each predicts here
| | H1 shared finite workspace | H2 modality-specific resources | H3 dynamic routing (readout = forthcoming answer; facts retrieved from source tokens on demand) |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1, non-target items at the decision position, B (any position may be queried) vs A (position p will be queried) | more readable under B (held as potentially relevant) | same as H1 within a modality | **no difference**: only the queried item is prepared |
| E2, visual accuracy and visual-target readability when the text list becomes relevant (V+T) vs irrelevant (V) | decreases, more with load k | unchanged (separate budgets) | unchanged unless attention is diverted; no J-mediated change |
| E2, causal swaps of a slot's state at the read layers | the J-readable part of the slot state moves the answer; competing facts share it | the J part moves the answer; no cross-modal competition | the full state moves the answer; the J part is not specifically effective |

## Stimuli (one generator, seed 0, held in `e0_stimuli`)
- Vocabulary: COCO classes with single-token names and distinct appearance — dog, cat, car, bus, chair, clock, cup, bird, train, horse,
  bicycle, airplane, boat, bed, pizza, laptop (16; any whose crop bank or k = 1 recognition fails is dropped and logged).
- Crop bank: COCO val2017 instances, not crowd, bbox ≥ 96 px on both sides, 10% margin, fitted into 184×184 on light grey; 12 per class.
- Image rendering: fixed canvas, fixed slots with a drawn slot number: E1 = 4 slots (448×448, 2×2 cells of 224 = 7×7 tokens each);
  E2 = 6 image slots (672×448, 3×2 cells). Empty slots stay grey with their number. Load varies the occupied slots, never the canvas.
- Text rendering of the same scene: "Position 1: dog; Position 2: chair; …" (same objects, same positions).
- Counterbalancing: target position rotated; class identities drawn without replacement per scene; for C/V+T the yes/no answer is balanced
  by planting a duplicate class in half the scenes; absent-class null = classes not in the scene.

## E1 — Does relevance change what J-space holds? (image and text renderings, 64 scenes, k = 4)
Prompts (the cue precedes the scene; the question is identical in A and B):
- A: "Study the scene. You will be asked about position {p}." [scene] "What object is in position {p}? Answer with one noun."
- B: "Study the scene. Any position may be queried." [scene] same question.
- C: "Study the scene. You will need to combine two positions." [scene] "Do positions {p} and {q} contain the same type of object? Answer yes or no."
Measurements (every read layer of the notebook band; best rank over layers; J-lens and logit lens; null = absent classes):
- accuracy and p(correct noun) (A, B); p(yes) and accuracy (C);
- readability (top-10) of every present item at its **own slot tokens** and at the **decision position** (last prompt token), per condition.
Preregistered reads:
- P1 selection: Δ = mean readability at the decision position of the **non-target** present items under B minus under A. Δ ≥ 0.15 with the
  95% scene-bootstrap interval above 0 → relevance admits items beyond the forthcoming answer (against H3). Δ inside [−0.05, 0.05]
  (equivalence) → the decision-position readout is answer preparation (H3).
- P2 matched output: target-item readability at the decision position equal in A and B (equivalence region ±0.1); if not, P1 is confounded.
- P3 source invariance: own-slot readability unchanged between A and B (reported, equivalence ±0.1).
- C is analysed separately: readability of both queried items at the decision position vs the other two.
Gates: single-fact accuracy at k = 4 ≥ 0.85 (image) and ≥ 0.95 (text) under A, else the rendering is "not seen" and its lens numbers are
not read; absent-class readability at the decision position ≤ 0.2 (else the top-10 rule is saturated and the rank AUROC is used instead).

## E2 — Cross-modal competition and causal interchange (64 base scenes; k ∈ {1, 2, 4, 6} occupied slots per source)
Each scene: 6 image slots with k objects and a 6-item text list with k *different* objects (R uses the same objects in both).
Arms (question targets rotated; same/different balanced):
- V0: image only — "What is in image slot {q}?"      V: image + text list (distractors) — same question
- T0: text only — "What is in text slot {q}?"        T: text + image (distractors) — same question
- VT: both relevant — "Does image slot {q} contain the same class as text slot {r}? Answer yes or no."
- R: image + identical text list — the V question (redundant information)
Estimands: interference I_T→V(k) = Acc_V0 − Acc_V (irrelevant text) and Acc_V0 − Acc_VT-component; same for I_V→T; readability of the
queried fact at its source tokens and at the decision position per arm; rule: interference when the drop ≥ 0.10 with interval above 0
at k = 6; none when inside [−0.05, 0.05]. R vs V: redundancy helps (≥ 0.05) / no effect.
Causal interchange (k ∈ {1, 4}; 32 donor/receiver pairs differing in one image slot's object; read layers = the notebook band):
- arms: full slot-state swap (positive control, must move p(donor object) by ≥ 0.5 on ≥ 0.8 of pairs, else uninterpretable); J-readable
  256-d part (top-256 right singular directions of W_U J_L); random 256-d part (same dimension, same hook); complement (non-J); non-target
  slot swap from a second donor (specificity, must be ≤ 0.05).
- outcomes: Δp(donor object) for the V question; Δp(yes) for the VT question whose answer depends on that slot (cross-modal interchange).
- rules: the J part carries a causally effective fact when Δp_J ≥ Δp_random + 0.2 with intervals apart; competition is J-mediated when
  the J-part effect on VT differs between the k = 1 and k = 4 scenes by ≥ 0.15 (reported, not a headline).
Abort: positive control fails → report, no subspace claims. Everything saved per scene with ids, seeds, prompts, lens path, model revision.

## T1 — transport cost by modality (new cell, rules frozen 14 Oct 01:35 before any run)
Question: when the same facts arrive as a picture and as text, is the J-readable part of the *evidence tokens* equally necessary?

## Audit against the goals (14 Oct 03:50, before any G1 data) and the realignment

What the ledger holds, stated without packaging. (a) One lens direction, the answer word's own, is necessary for a visual answer on three sizes of one family (4B −0.98, 2B, Qwen3.5-27B −0.56 [−0.72, −0.43]; another word's direction 0.00 to 0.01). This is close to what the lens construction promises (the direction is the linearised gradient of that word's logit) and is not a discovery; its transfer from text-fitted directions to image tokens is the non-trivial part. (b) The 256-direction lens part is necessary on the 2B and 4B and not on either 27B (#23, #37, and Qwen3.5-27B, version-clean: J − random −0.07 [−0.21, 0.09]); the generic verbalizable subspace loses its privilege with scale while the single name direction keeps it. (c) A 256-direction slice of the picture state, lens or random, lets a receiver name what was seen and not use it in a rule; the full state does both (F1 #45, F2: letter 0.78 / 0.80 vs animal 0.52 / 0.41, full 0.92 / 0.94). The lens part is not special in that comparison. (d) Printed words depend on the lens part more than photographs (T1) and, as a donor, carry the first letter (0.86) but not the category (0.48, positive control failed, F2). (e) No central store at the question or answer positions (#2, #3). (f) The canvas and SCAM swaps (A1, A2) are Debanshu's swap with controls and a dose curve.

What is missing: the one sentence with a hard-to-fake consequence. The original sentence (a capacity-limited reportable workspace) died at #1 to #3. The replacement, "saying is cheaper than using", is supported by (c) and (d) only in the sufficiency direction and with no lens-specific content, and the paper-level question it raises has not been asked: does the model use what it sees through the direction that lets it say it? The lens direction of an object's name on its own image tokens is the only lens quantity that stays causal at every scale tested. If removing that one direction stops the model from using the object in a rule that never asks for the name, then a vision-language model reasons about what it sees through the name, and the verbal handle is the interface between perception and use (the workspace account of Gurnee et al. extended to vision at rank one, and F1 says the handle alone is not the fact). If the rules survive, the model uses what it can no longer name, which is anomia or blindsight in a model, and the lens reads the saying channel only (tool blindness for lens-based audits of vision models). Either answer is reportable; neither is in the literature as far as the novelty ledger goes; and F2's printed-word donor hints that a printed word is a handle without a referent.

Decision. No more N1, T1 or A-type iteration. One experiment, G1, with the rules below, on the 4B and the 2B tonight; the Gemma-4-31B N1 stays queued only because it carries the rank-1 arm (the handle in a second family at 31B). The report is rewritten around (a) to (d) as observations and G1 as the test, with no theory presented as established.

## Rubric self-grade (21 Oct 13:10, after rows 97 to 108; no averaging; a gate is listed only where it applies)

Three sentences the rubric asks for (CL to CLIII). Scientific: we provide evidence that a vision-language model reads an object's
image tokens at two stations, an early one for every decision about the object and a late one for its name, and that the same
heads read the object when it arrives as a typed word. Mechanistic: our causal evidence supports that the reader heads' outputs
carry the object without its channel (written from the word's run into the picture's run they move the decision on 0.98 and the
name on 0.95 of scenes), that the channel is carried by other heads at the middle layers, and that the model's report of whether it
saw or read the object follows those heads (report changed with the name held, 16 of 24 scenes one way and none the other).
Cognitive analogy: the task instantiates the source-monitoring distinction between item and source information in that the object
and its channel are delivered by different components and the channel can be lost with the object kept; it differs in that the
"source" here is an internal routing signal and not a memory of an external event, and in that the object was placed by
intervention. Safety: kept as implication (a model's account of where its content came from can be wrong while the internal
signal that predicts the error is readable), with no monitoring demonstration.

Three contributions (CCLX): (1) the two stations, by patching and knockout, across Qwen 2B, 4B, 0.8B and Gemma E4B, with the
decision-against-copy rule tested on held-out questions (sixteen-way identification) and a held-out model; (2) the readers named,
necessary and sufficient, shared between a picture and a word, with the content interchangeable across modalities; (3) the channel
carried apart from the content, and the model's source report following the channel-carrying heads.

Cognitive mapping (CXXII): human construct, source monitoring (Johnson et al. 1993): item memory against source attribution as an
inference over cues. Machine operationalization: the object delivered by the reader heads (content) against the channel component
in other heads (source), read out by the "seen or read" question. Similarity: both can be present without the other; the
attribution follows cues (here, which heads delivered the content). Difference: no memory over time, one forward pass; the source
here is an architectural routing signal; the object was injected, so the "true" source is the experimenter's intervention.
